A digitized book is only as valuable as its discoverability. Without accurate, standardized metadata, even the highest-resolution digital collections become invisible in modern library systems.

## What is Bibliographic Metadata Extraction?

Metadata extraction is the systematic harvesting, structuring, and enrichment of publication data directly from book covers, title pages, colophons, and index registries. Key metadata fields include:

1. **Descriptive Data**: Title, subtitle, author authority, contributors, edition details, publisher, and publication year.
2. **Identification Standards**: ISBN-10, ISBN-13, ISSN, DOI, and Library of Congress Control Numbers (LCCN).
3. **Classification & Subject Headings**: Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC), Library of Congress Classification (LCC), and BISAC subject categories.
4. **Structural Records**: Hierarchical tables of contents, preface, chapter milestones, and index page mappings.

## Standard Schemas: Dublin Core, MARC21 & ONIX

Depending on whether your digital archive serves academic repositories, public library consortiums, or retail eBook distribution channels, metadata must conform to specific standards:

- **MARC21**: The backbone of institutional library catalog systems worldwide.
- **Dublin Core**: Lightweight, flexible vocabulary ideal for web archives and open repositories.
- **ONIX for Books**: The global XML publishing industry standard for commercial and retail distribution.

Automating extraction while retaining human editorial review guarantees 100% catalog integrity and effortless patron searchability.
